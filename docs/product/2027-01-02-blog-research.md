# Talko AI — January–February 2027 blog campaign

Research checked November 6, 2026. Nineteen finished articles extend the existing December 31 release, every three days from January 3 to February 26. Publication: **14:00 UTC / 19:30 IST**. The same initial Europe/US East/India overlap is retained; no site analytics establish it as an optimum. The application clock releases the articles, including the blog index, metadata and sitemap, after deployment. The owner portal retains Preview, Publish now and Reschedule.

## Selection method and six-month outlook

Forecast window: **November 2026–May 2027**. These are editorial hypotheses about durable buyer problems, not predictions of future news, search volumes or rankings. Every topic was checked against the existing published, November and December inventories. The distinction is the task and search intent, not a new version of an old title. We favoured current product fit, an actionable buyer problem, a distinct operating question, an appropriate primary source and relevance beyond a seasonal promotion.

Evidence combines current first-party platform/provider documentation, 2026 business/customer research and sampled English-language search results for knowledge, booking, response-time, broadcast, Messenger, lead, handover and multilingual workflows. Search results show competing pages and active vocabulary, not demand magnitude. No Search Console query export, verified GA4 segment, paid volume estimate or regional keyword panel was available. Competition is material; smaller specific workflow queries are more defensible than promising broad “AI chatbot” rankings. International reach is English-language; translated pages are outside this campaign.

Current evidence includes Meta’s June 2026 business-agent expansion, Salesforce’s May 2026 service research, October 2026 shopping analysis, operational provider references and primary security guidance. Older sources are labelled as such. We excluded future product announcements, unbuilt live voice calling, guaranteed autonomous purchasing, universal shipping integrations, Facebook auto-hide/delete claims and invented customer results.

## SEO, AEO and GEO implementation

The changes preserve server-rendered article text, clean headings, canonical URLs, real organisation authorship, article-specific sharing images and publication-gated sitemap entries. New pages add direct introductory answers, three concise takeaways, worked examples, tables, an interactive checklist, visible research-check dates and citations next to supported claims. Related reading favours explicitly related published articles and the same category; unreleased articles do not appear in that recommendation. The mobile contents menu and desktop reading guide use real anchor links. The optional client enhancements do not hide the article text.

BlogPosting structured data includes language and the same source URLs displayed to readers. Publication and modification dates stay tied to the article’s actual schedule/override; a research-check date is shown separately. FAQ content remains available to readers, but the blog does not emit FAQPage markup or promise FAQ rich results. No special “AI schema,” keyword stuffing, hidden text, invented author credentials or fabricated evidence is added.

Google says normal SEO principles still apply to AI Overviews/AI Mode, important content should be available as text and structured data should match visible content. Eligibility does not guarantee indexing or inclusion. We do not claim that special files or a publication hour force rankings. [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

Original operating examples and product limits provide value beyond summarising surveys. The article length reflects what the workflow requires, not a supposed preferred Google word count. [Google: people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content). Article metadata follows the [Article guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article).

## Lead path and measurement

Every article has an article-attributed signup link, pricing/feature links and a specific enquiry invitation. The existing blog CTA event includes the campaign `2027-01-02-operations`; successful form submissions retain the article attribution. A click is not a lead. No new email list, pop-up gate or unbuilt download offer is introduced.

Review article indexing, impressions, relevant query coverage and clicks in Search Console when account access is available. Use the existing analytics for qualified landing visits, CTA clicks and successful enquiries, then have sales record fit and accepted next action. Compare equal observation windows because the last article is younger. Review supported AI citation data in [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/); citations are not rankings or proof of leads. This task does not connect webmaster accounts or invent missing conversion instrumentation.

Initial review points: after each page has had 14 days for technical/discovery checks, at 30 days for query/visitor quality, and at 60–90 days for qualified enquiry outcomes. These are operating checkpoints, not promised traffic or conversion windows. If there is insufficient data, report that rather than infer a win.

## Editorial freshness

The CSV includes a source-refresh date seven days before each release. A person should recheck changing platform details and product availability, update substantive changes honestly, and use the owner portal to reschedule if the article is not ready. These reminders are a documented review process, not an unattended research job. The publication schedule does not rewrite news or re-verify sources automatically.
